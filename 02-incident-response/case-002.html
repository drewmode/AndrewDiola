// 01
Overview

This case picks up where CASE-001 left off. A dashboard full of alerts means nothing if there's no process behind it — so this lab focused on the workflow that turns a raw detection into a tracked, resolved incident.

The goal was to practice the judgment calls a Tier 1 analyst makes daily: which alerts deserve a ticket, how to prioritize a stack of open cases, and how to write a report that's useful to whoever picks it up next.

// 03
Methodology
Step 01

Convert Alerts to Tickets

Took the findings surfaced in CASE-001 and logged each one as a discrete ticket instead of a one-off search result.

Step 02

Triage & Prioritize

Scored each ticket by asset criticality, likelihood of compromise, and potential business impact — not just alert volume.

Step 03

Write Incident Reports

For the highest-priority tickets, wrote structured reports: what happened, when, what indicators were involved, and what to do next.

Step 04

Hand-Off Simulation

Reviewed each report as if a different analyst had to pick it up cold — checking it made sense without extra context.

// 05
Key Findings
Not all alerts deserve equal attention: applying a consistent priority model surfaced a small number of tickets that mattered far more than raw alert count suggested.
Documentation is a deliverable: a technically correct finding is only useful if the write-up lets the next analyst act on it without re-doing the investigation.
Triage is a skill, not a formality: consistently applying the same criteria to every ticket made prioritization faster and more defensible over time.

Reflection

The hardest part of this case wasn't writing the reports — it was resisting the urge to treat every alert as equally urgent. Building a consistent priority model forced me to slow down and actually think about impact instead of just reacting to volume.

I came away from this with a much better sense of why documentation is treated as a first-class deliverable in a SOC, not an afterthought. A finding nobody can act on isn't really a finding — it's just noise with extra steps.
